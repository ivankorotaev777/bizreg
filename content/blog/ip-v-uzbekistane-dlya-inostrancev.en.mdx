<Lead>
A foreigner can register as a sole proprietor (IP) in Uzbekistan, but only if they actually live in the country (you need residence registration, a PINFL and a digital signature); without residency the status cannot be obtained. It is one of the most common and most confusing questions, because the answer hides two traps. The first is terminological: the abbreviation "IP" means two completely different things in Uzbekistan. The second is practical: formally an IP is available to a foreigner, but on terms that lead most people to choose a different form. Let's untangle it step by step, with no myths and grounded in primary sources.
</Lead>

<Callout type="warning" title="Two meanings of «IP» — don't mix them up">
In Uzbek business language "IP" is decoded in two ways, and this is a constant source of mistakes:

- **IP = sole proprietor (individual entrepreneur)** — a natural person with entrepreneur status. This is what the article is about.
- **IP = foreign enterprise** — this is an **LLC with 100% foreign capital**, a full legal entity. It has nothing to do with sole proprietorship.

From here on, "IP" means the **sole proprietor**, and we call the 100%-capital form a "foreign enterprise".
</Callout>

## The short answer: yes, but with a condition

A common myth goes: "an IP is simply not available to foreigners in Uzbekistan." That is inaccurate. The law **does allow** a foreign citizen to obtain sole proprietor status — but only if they **actually live in Uzbekistan**. The key word here is residence, not citizenship.

In practice this means a chain of mandatory steps: temporary residence registration, a PINFL (personal ID number), then a digital signature (EDS) and filing the application. Without the first item — residence registration — the whole path is impossible.

## What does a foreigner need to open an IP in Uzbekistan?

The main difference from registering an LLC: to become a sole proprietor, a foreigner must be **"tied" to the country** — the system requires their physical presence and registration as a resident person.

## What tax does an IP pay in Uzbekistan in 2026?

Here is the good news for micro-business. From 1 January 2026, sole proprietors and self-employed with annual turnover **up to 1 billion soum** pay a single turnover tax of **1%** (Decree PP-247 of 12.08.2025). That is noticeably lower than previous rates and makes the IP attractive for very small activity.

<Callout type="note" title="What happens above the threshold">
If an IP's annual turnover exceeds 1 billion soum, the favourable 1% rate no longer applies — the regime changes, and the gap with an LLC narrows. That is why an IP pays off precisely while turnover stays small. Exact transition rules are on [soliq.uz](https://soliq.uz) and [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/7681524).
</Callout>

## IP limits people learn about too late

On paper an IP looks simpler and cheaper than an LLC. But the form has "ceilings" that a foreign entrepreneur hits more often than expected.

<ProsCons>
  <Pros title="Strengths of an IP">

  - Low 1% turnover tax for micro-business (2026)
  - Fast registration — literally 1 day
  - Minimal bureaucracy and reporting
  - A bank account is optional if you work alone
  - Easy start for those already living in Uzbekistan

  </Pros>
  <Cons title="IP limits for a foreigner">

  - You must actually live in the country (registration/permit)
  - Only 88 permitted activity types
  - No more than 5 hired employees
  - Liability with all personal assets
  - No access to IT Park resident status and benefits

  </Cons>
</ProsCons>

It is worth stressing **personal liability** separately. An IP is liable for obligations with all their property, whereas an LLC participant generally risks only their contribution to the capital. For a business with contracts, advances and hiring, that is a serious argument.

<Callout type="warning" title="Working without registration means a fine">
You cannot run business activity without registering an IP (or a legal entity): the penalty is 10% of the income received, but no less than 1 million soum (Tax Code, art. 219). So "I'll work without status for now" is a bad idea. Verify on [lex.uz](https://lex.uz) and [soliq.uz](https://soliq.uz).
</Callout>

## Why do foreigners more often choose an LLC instead of an IP?

When we help clients decide, in most cases the choice leans toward an LLC — and most often toward a **foreign enterprise** (an LLC with 100% foreign capital). The reasons are practical.

<IconGrid>
  <Feature icon="globe" title="No need to relocate">An LLC can be registered without living in Uzbekistan. An IP cannot be set up without residing in the country — this is the main dividing line.</Feature>
  <Feature icon="building" title="No growth ceilings">Any activities and unlimited hiring. The business won't have to "change its shell" once it outgrows the IP.</Feature>
  <Feature icon="rocket" title="Access to IT Park">Only a legal entity can become an IT Park resident and use its tax benefits — decisive for IT.</Feature>
  <Feature icon="bank" title="Trust of banks and partners">An LLC is more familiar to banks, investors and large counterparties than a non-resident IP — less friction at compliance.</Feature>
</IconGrid>

This does not mean an IP is "worse". For an entrepreneur who already lives in Tashkent, works alone and fits within the activity list, an IP with a 1% tax can be ideal. But as soon as there is a remote owner, a team to hire, an IT focus or scale-up plans, the logic swings toward an LLC.

## How to choose: a quick guide

To avoid getting lost in the nuances, keep this simple decision logic.

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Do you live in Uzbekistan?">
  If **no** — the IP is ruled out immediately, leaving an LLC or foreign enterprise. If **yes** — an IP is at least possible, move on.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="Is your activity on the IP list?">
  The list is limited to 88 types. If your activity is not there — again an LLC. If it is — the IP stays in play.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="How many people will you hire?">
  An IP cannot exceed 5 employees by law. A larger team means an LLC.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Do you need IT Park or outside investment?">
  If yes — you need a legal entity. An IP is not built for IT Park residency or bringing in investors through shares.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="tip" title="A practical rule">
Solo work + living in the country + a simple service activity = an IP at 1% can pay off. Remote ownership, a team, IT, scale, investors = an LLC (most often a foreign enterprise with 100% capital). Borderline cases are best reviewed individually.
</Callout>

<KeyTakeaways title="The essentials in a minute">

- "IP" in Uzbekistan is either a sole proprietor (a person) or a foreign enterprise (an LLC with 100% capital); don't confuse them.
- A foreigner **can** become an IP, but only if they **live** in the country: residence registration, a PINFL and a digital signature are required.
- The IP tax in 2026 is 1% on turnover up to 1 billion soum (PP-247); above the threshold the regime changes.
- An IP is limited to 88 activity types, 5 employees and personal liability for debts.
- Most foreigners choose an LLC / foreign enterprise: it can be opened remotely, has no ceilings and gives access to IT Park.

</KeyTakeaways>
