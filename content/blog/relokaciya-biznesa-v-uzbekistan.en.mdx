## What to move and how to structure it

Relocation is not "moving the whole legal entity." Your Russian LLC stays Russian; in Uzbekistan you create a **new company** and move there the functions that hit international-payment and sanctions limits.

The typical relocation logic:

- **Contracts with foreign suppliers and clients** — signed on behalf of the Uzbek company so payments flow through an Uzbek bank.
- **A settlement "hub"** — the Uzbek entity as the center for inbound and outbound international payments.
- **IT and digital operations** — development, SaaS and marketing services are conveniently run through an IT Park resident.
- **Team** — staff can be hired locally; relocating people is planned separately from relocating money.

<Callout type="warning" title="Do not confuse relocation with 'moving' your Russian LLC">
You cannot transfer the Russian legal entity itself into Uzbekistan — you register a new company there. The Russian business keeps existing and is taxed under Russian law. The structure of the two companies and the movement of funds between them must be built correctly under the currency and tax legislation of both countries.
</Callout>

## Which form to choose: LLC or foreign enterprise

Here is the key difference from Russia that trips up almost everyone: **a foreigner cannot register as a sole proprietor (IP)** in Uzbekistan. The familiar Russian path of "I'll open a sole proprietorship on a simplified regime" is not available here.

Two forms are available:

<CompareGrid>
  <CompareCard title="LLC (with foreign participation)" badge="Flexible">
  A limited liability company that includes a foreign participant. Suitable if you plan joint ownership with local partners or a mixed structure.
  </CompareCard>
  <CompareCard title="Foreign enterprise" badge="100% foreign" highlight>
  An LLC with **100% foreign capital** — the optimal choice for a relocator who wants full control of the company without a local co-founder.
  </CompareCard>
</CompareGrid>

<Callout type="info" title="'IP' in Uzbekistan is not what it is in Russia">
In the Uzbek context the abbreviation IP often means "foreign enterprise" (an LLC with 100% foreign capital), not "individual entrepreneur." Because of this overlap relocators get confused regularly. Remember: there is no individual-entrepreneur status available to a foreigner.
</Callout>

## The bank account: the key and most "physical" step

The account is the whole point — it is what closes the international-payments pain. And it is exactly where presence is most often required.

## IT Park: the main argument for digital businesses

If you are moving IT development, SaaS, a product team or digital services — IT Park residency may be the deciding factor for choosing Uzbekistan.

<Callout type="tip" title="IT Park resident benefits">
Exemption from **profit tax, VAT, social tax and turnover tax** until **1 January 2028**, and **employee personal income tax of 7.5%**. Conditions and residency requirements are on [it-park.uz](https://it-park.uz).
</Callout>

For a development team this means a tax burden several times lower than under the standard regime, plus clear rules for hiring local specialists.

## Residence permit — in brief

Company registration and a residence permit are two different procedures. Opening a company does not grant a residence permit automatically, but it lays the groundwork for further legalization of the founder's and investor's stay. Check the grounds, timelines and document set for a residence permit on [gov.uz](https://gov.uz) — categories and conditions are updated periodically (accurate as of 2026-06-15).
