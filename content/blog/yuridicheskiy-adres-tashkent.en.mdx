## What is a legal address in Tashkent?

A legal address is the official registered location of a company, stated at state registration and entered into the register. It is not just a line in the documents: this is the address by which the state and the market "see" your company.

## Guarantee letter and address registration

To state an address as legal it is not enough to simply "write it in" the application. You need a lawful basis to use those premises — and this is where the guarantee letter comes in.

A **guarantee letter** is a document from the owner of the premises confirming consent to provide the address to the company for registration. It shows the registering authority and the bank that the company has a real right to be at this address, rather than a "paper" registration out of thin air.

<Callout type="info" title="Why a guarantee letter is needed">
A guarantee letter ties the company to specific premises and their owner. Without a confirmed basis for the address, registration and later checks become harder — the bank and government bodies want to see that the business actually "lives" somewhere.
</Callout>

After registration the address becomes the company's official detail. If the actual location changes, the address must be updated promptly — a discrepancy between the register and reality creates extra risk during checks.

## Why is a "mass" legal address risky?

The most common way to over-economize on registration is taking a cheap "mass" address where dozens, or even hundreds, of companies are already listed. At the start it looks harmless, but this is exactly where problems with the bank and the tax authority grow from.

<Callout type="warning" title="A 'mass' address attracts questions">
When many firms are registered at a single address, banks and government bodies treat it as a risk signal. This can lead to refusal or delay in opening an account, heightened compliance and extra scrutiny of the company. Saving on the address often turns into much greater losses of time and nerves.
</Callout>

The logic is simple: the address is one of the markers used to judge whether a business is real. A "clean" address works for you; a "mass" one works against you.

## Legal address for different regimes and forms

Any company needs an address regardless of the tax regime and form you choose. Here it is important to remember a key difference from the Russian model.

<Callout type="info" title="Sole proprietor — only with residency in Uzbekistan">
A [sole proprietor in Uzbekistan for foreigners](/en/blog/ip-v-uzbekistane-dlya-inostrancev) is available only with residency in the country (registration by place of residence or a residence permit) and with limits, so a non-resident usually opens an LLC or a foreign enterprise (an LLC with 100% foreign capital) — and each of these forms also requires a legal address for registration.
</Callout>

The most common scenarios for choosing an address:

- **An LLC on the general or simplified regime** — needs a correct registered location tied to a tax office.
- **A foreign enterprise (100% foreign capital)** — the address is just as mandatory as for a regular LLC.
- **Companies focused on a bank account and international payments** — here the "cleanliness" of the address is especially critical for compliance.
- **IT and digital companies** — even when aiming for preferential regimes, the legal address remains a mandatory requirement.

Verify the exact requirements for the address and permitted types of premises on [my.gov.uz](https://my.gov.uz) and [lex.uz](https://lex.uz) — the procedure is updated from time to time (accurate as of 2026-06-16).
