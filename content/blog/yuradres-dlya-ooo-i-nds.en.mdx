## Why is a legal address for an LLC and VAT checked twice?

A legal address is a company's official place of location that goes into the state register and ties the entity to a specific tax office. The key point founders miss: the same address passes two different checks at two different moments — treating it as just a line in a form is dangerous.

The first check is at LLC registration. Here the place of location is a mandatory detail: without it the entity simply cannot be entered in the register. At this stage the registering authority checks that the address is valid and that the company has a basis to use it.

The second check is at VAT registration. When turnover reaches the threshold, or you register for VAT voluntarily, the address is in focus again. And the tax office's logic shifts: a VAT payer issues electronic invoices and claims deductions, so the state wants to be sure there is real activity behind the address, not a paper point.

## Address for LLC registration: the baseline

At registration, the address's job is to tie the new entity to a specific place. It is the foundation without which the next steps are impossible.

<Steps>
  <Step n={1} title="Address is a mandatory detail">
  You cannot register an LLC without stating a place of location. This is a direct requirement of the Regulation on state registration of business entities.
  </Step>
  <Step n={2} title="A basis for the premises is required">
  The right to use the address is proven by a document from the owner: a letter of guarantee, a lease agreement or a title document.
  </Step>
  <Step n={3} title="Link to a tax office">
  The address attaches the company to a specific tax office, where notices and requests are sent.
  </Step>
  <Step n={4} title="Address goes into the register">
  After registration the address becomes an official detail visible to counterparties, the bank and government bodies.
  </Step>
</Steps>

At this level the requirements are relatively soft: what matters is a correct address and a confirmed right to use it. Many companies register and operate for years without facing a deeper address check. But the moment a business reaches VAT, the picture changes.

## Address for VAT: a higher level of scrutiny

VAT registration moves you into a different weight class in terms of tax administration. A VAT payer works with invoices, output and input tax, and deductions — the very tools that, unfortunately, fraudulent schemes also use. So the tax office pays closer attention to how real a company is, and the address is one of the first signals.

<Callout type="info" title="Why the address matters specifically for VAT">
VAT status grants the right to issue invoices and to deduct input tax. To rule out fictitious transactions, the state checks that there is real activity behind the company at a real address. An empty or mass address works against you here far more than at ordinary registration.
</Callout>

It is important to understand the link with the 2026 changes. From 1 June 2026 the mandatory VAT registration threshold was raised to 12,000 BRV (about 5 billion soums), and a voluntary 6% rate appeared for trade, catering and services alongside the standard 12%. This means part of the business stays out of VAT longer — but those who do reach it fall under closer administration. ## Why a mass address is especially risky under VAT

A mass address is premises where dozens, or even hundreds, of companies are registered. At registration it may pass unnoticed. But for a VAT payer it becomes a constant source of risk.

<Callout type="warning" title="Mass address and VAT — a bad combination">
At a mass address the tax office checks the reality of activity more often. For a VAT payer this can put invoices and deductions in doubt, and your counterparties risk being unable to deduct your VAT. Saving on an address at the start turns into far bigger losses once the company reaches VAT.
</Callout>

The logic is simple: the address is one of the markers of a real business. When a company becomes a VAT payer, the cost of a dirty address rises sharply, because deductions, counterparties and reputation in the tax office's eyes are all attached to it.

## How to prove a legal address

Proving the address is the documentary basis that ties the company to specific premises. It is needed both at registration and because the tax office may match the address against reality at the VAT stage.

  <DocRow doc="Letter of guarantee" who="Premises owner" note="Confirms consent to provide the address for company registration." />
  <DocRow doc="Lease agreement" who="Landlord and company" note="Shows a real, ongoing basis to use the premises." highlight />
  <DocRow doc="Title document" who="Owning company" note="The strongest proof, if the premises belong to the company." />
  <DocRow doc="Update in the register" who="Company" note="A change of place of location must be reported to the registering authorities." />

A separate point is notifying a change of address. The Regulation on state registration expressly provides for notifying the registering authorities of a change of address (place of location). For an ordinary company a mismatch between register and reality is an annoyance; for a VAT payer it is a direct reason for questions about invoices and deductions.

## What does the tax office check in a legal address?

At VAT registration and during operations the tax office may assess the address against several markers of reality. Understanding these markers helps you choose the right address in advance.

<IconGrid>
  <Feature icon="building" title="Reality of premises">
  Whether the premises physically exist and are used, rather than existing only on paper.
  </Feature>
  <Feature icon="doc" title="Basis for the address">
  Whether there is a letter of guarantee, a lease agreement or a title document.
  </Feature>
  <Feature icon="shield" title="Mass nature of the address">
  How many other companies are registered at the same address.
  </Feature>
  <Feature icon="scale" title="Match with the register">
  Whether the address in the register matches the actual place of business.
  </Feature>
</IconGrid>

Check the exact review procedure and the list of acceptable grounds on [soliq.uz](https://soliq.uz) and [lex.uz](https://www.lex.uz/acts/3111342) — it is updated periodically (current as of 2026-06-15).
